Positioning

Who it's for and how it sounds

The brand borrows its look from documents buyers already trust: manufacturer spec sheets, capacity plates and engineering drawings. Lift tables are bought on numbers, so the numbers lead.

What it is
Manufacturers, product lines and model numbers for lift tables, plus the distributors who sell them.
Who it's for
People who specify or buy lift tables: plant and maintenance managers, safety and ergonomics leads, purchasing. Also distributors who want those buyers' quote requests.
What it offers
Compare lift tables by capacity, platform size and model, then get a quote from a distributor.
Design idea
The spec sheet. Steel neutrals, one safety colour, figures in monospace, square corners with a single chamfer.

Personality

  • Precise

    Exact figures, real units, model numbers exactly as the manufacturer prints them. Never clinical or cold.

  • Plainspoken

    Short sentences that tell a buyer what a table does and what to check. No hype, no superlatives.

  • Useful

    Every element helps someone compare or act. Decoration earns its place by carrying information.

Colour

Steel neutrals, one safety colour

The neutrals are greys with a slight blue cast, like cold-rolled steel. Safety yellow is the only brand colour. It is paint, never ink: use it for fills, never for text on white. It reaches only 1.8:1 against white, so it can't be read as text.

Typography

Two families, three roles

Display and text

Aa Gg 49

Archivo, a grotesque with a variable width axis. Headlines run wide (112–122%) and heavy (700–800) with tight tracking. Body text uses normal width at 400–650.

Data and labels

L-4436

IBM Plex Mono for model numbers, capacities, dimensions and labels. Every character is the same width, so columns of figures line up. Labels are uppercase with 0.08em tracking.

Signature details

Four things only this brand does

The rating plate

Key figures sit in bordered cells with one chamfered corner, like the capacity plate on real equipment. Use it for a page's headline facts: series, capacity, model count, headquarters.

  • SeriesALPHA
  • Capacity2,200–6,600 lbs

The dimension rule

Section headings sit on a hairline with end ticks, like a dimension line on a drawing. The optional link on the right reads as a callout.

Product lines

All product lines →

The chamfer

One 45° cut on the top-right corner. It appears on the badge, the rating plate, the quote form and listing badges, and nowhere else. Every other corner is square, with a 2 px radius at most.

The technical drawing

Illustrations are side elevations in ink line with dimension callouts in mono caps. Yellow goes only on the part that lifts. On the home page this drawing raises its platform once as the page loads.

Writing

Figures, names and claims

  • Capacity in lbs, dimensions in inches. Fractions to the nearest 1/16: 8-3/4 in, not 8.75".
  • Platform length first: 31 x 53 in. Round decks: 43 in dia.
  • Model numbers exactly as the manufacturer prints them, set in mono. Never reformat or abbreviate them.
  • Sentence case for headings, buttons and labels. Proper names keep their own capitals: ALPHA, EZ Loader.
  • Buttons say what happens: "Request a quote", "Find a distributor", "List your company".
  • State only what a source supports. No invented ratings, availability, prices or "best". Say "listed" rather than "every".
  • Pallet positioners that are lift tables may use "pallet positioner" in the title, but the copy describes the lifting table.

Imagery

Photos, logos and drawings

  • Product photos come from the manufacturer, with permission, on white or light grey. Square-on or three-quarter view, no watermarks.
  • Brand logos come from the manufacturer and sit on a white panel with padding, never recoloured.
  • Illustrations follow the technical drawing: ink lines, yellow only on the lifting part. Each equipment category has one; see Category illustrations.
  • No AI-generated pictures of real products. They won't match the real model, and buyers compare real models.
  • No stock photos of people pointing at tablets or shaking hands, and no decorative hazard stripes.

Category illustrations

One drawing per table type

Every equipment category has a drawing of that type of table, seen from the side. They appear in the table type tiles on the home page and on category pages, and at the top of each category page.

The drawings are written in code, not drawn by hand. generators/category-images.php builds all of them from a small set of shared parts, so every table uses the same line weights, colours and proportions, and changing a rule changes every drawing at once. Scissor geometry is calculated rather than eyeballed: the legs pivot on fixed pins at one end and roll at the other, and the span follows from the leg length and the lift height, just as it does on a real table.

Anatomy of a drawing

Construction rules

ElementRule
Canvas480 × 320 px (3:2), transparent. The page supplies the drawing-grid background, so one file works on any surface.
ViewSide elevation, square-on. No perspective, no shading, no shadows.
GroundA 3 px Steel Ink line at y 276, over a 14 px band of 45° hatching in Line Strong #AAB4BD.
Outlines3 px Steel Ink #18212A, square ends. Filled shapes are white.
YellowSafety Yellow #F5B800 on the part that lifts, and nothing else: the deck, turntable or top plate.
Secondary partsGauge Grey #5C6975: cylinders, hoses, loads, pallets, pallet jacks, arrows and dimension lines. Lighter details in Line Strong.
Scissor legsAn 18 px ink bar with a 12 px white core, so crossed legs stay readable at thumbnail size. Heavy duty uses 26 / 16 px; low profile 12 / 7 px.
PinsFixed pins 6 px radius, rollers 8 px, centre pivot 7 px, all white with a 3 px outline. Heavy duty adds 2 px to each.
Dimension lineOne vertical line, floor to deck top, 1.5 px Gauge Grey with 18 px end ticks. On the right, or on the left when a power unit takes the right. Left out where height isn't the point: tilt tables show their angle instead, and floor level tables show the flush floor.
TextNone. Fonts don't load inside an image file, and a drawing without words works in any language. The category name is set beside the image by the page.
One idea eachEvery drawing adds a single feature to the standard table that makes its type recognisable. If a second feature is needed to tell two drawings apart, the first one isn't clear enough.

Adding or changing a drawing

  • Edit the generator, never the SVG. Build the new drawing from the shared parts in generators/category-images.php (scissor_table(), caster(), cylinder(), crate(), pallet(), dim_v()), then run php generators/category-images.php.
  • Name the file after the category slug: images/categories/scissor-lift-tables.svg, and give it a plain title such as "Scissor lift table". The title becomes the image's alt text.
  • Point the category at it with "image" in categories.json and run the category importer. Unchanged files are left alone; a changed file replaces the existing image in the media library, so every page picks it up.
  • A new niche site reuses the same parts and rules with its own equipment, and its own safety colour in place of yellow.

Niche sites

One theme, one safety colour per site

Every site in the network shares the theme, type, neutrals, chamfer and badge shape. Only three things change: the name, the equipment drawn inside the badge, and the safety colour. The colours come from the ANSI Z535.1 safety palette, so the sites read as a family. Red is kept for errors on every site.
